RAID Levels & Fault Tolerance

RAID 0, 1, 5, 6, and 10 architectures, capacity/parity math, hardware controllers, BBU/FBWC, and URE rebuilds.

DOMAIN SCOPE (VLAN 03): Everything inside this room belongs strictly to RAID (Redundant Array of Independent Disks) levels, capacity/fault math, hardware vs. software controllers, caching, and rebuild mechanics.
RAID Level Mechanism Min Drives Usable Capacity Fault Tolerance Write Penalty & Profile
RAID 0 Block-level Striping (No parity, no mirroring) 2 N × S (100%) 0 drives (Any single failure destroys array) 1× penalty. Maximum read/write speed; scratch disks/temp cache only.
RAID 1 Disk Mirroring (100% duplicate copy) 2 (N / 2) × S (50%) 1 drive per mirror pair 2× write penalty. Fast reads; ideal for OS boot volumes.
RAID 5 Block Striping + Single Distributed Parity 3 (N - 1) × S 1 drive simultaneous failure 4× write penalty. High read speed; risky on large multi-TB HDDs due to rebuild UREs.
RAID 6 Block Striping + Dual Distributed Parity 4 (N - 2) × S 2 drives simultaneous failure 6× write penalty. Survives a second drive failure during long multi-TB rebuilds.
RAID 10
(RAID 1+0)		 Striped set of Mirrored pairs 4 (even) (N / 2) × S (50%) 1 drive per mirror pair (up to N/2 if in different pairs) 2× write penalty. No parity calculation; top choice for high-IOPS databases.

Hardware RAID vs. Software RAID

  • Hardware RAID Controller: Dedicated PCIe card or motherboard controller with its own XOR parity processor and onboard DRAM cache; presents logical volumes directly to BIOS/OS.
  • BBU / FBWC (Cache Protection): Battery Backup Unit or Flash-Backed Write Cache preserves dirty write-back cache data in non-volatile flash during sudden power loss.
  • Write-Back vs. Write-Through: Write-back acknowledges writes immediately from controller cache (fast, requires BBU/FBWC); Write-through writes directly to disk before acknowledging (safe without battery, slower).
  • Software RAID: Managed by the host OS kernel (Windows Storage Spaces, Linux mdadm, ZFS RAID-Z); consumes host CPU cycles and cannot easily boot without OS driver support.

Hot Spares, Rebuilds & URE Risk

  • Dedicated vs. Global Hot Spare: Standby physical drive powered on and idle; automatically begins rebuilding a degraded array the instant an active drive fails (Dedicated = assigned to 1 array; Global = serves any array on controller).
  • Degraded State: Array remains online after a drive failure within its tolerance limit, operating at reduced read/write performance until rebuilt.
  • URE (Unrecoverable Read Error): During a multi-terabyte RAID 5 rebuild, reading 100% of remaining sectors can hit a latent bad sector, causing the entire RAID 5 rebuild to fail—why RAID 6 is mandatory for large-capacity HDDs.
  • Mixed Drive Sizing Rule: Mixing drive sizes in a standard RAID array truncates every drive to the capacity of the smallest drive in the set.
