Ohm's / Watt's Power Law Watts (W) = Volts (V) × Amps (A)

Redundant A/B Feed Rule Max Load per PDU ≤ 50% Never load either Feed A or Feed B past 50% capacity—if Feed A fails, Feed B instantly absorbs 100% of rack load.

Server Rack Architecture & Mounting Rules

  • 4-Post vs. 2-Post Racks: Heavy deep servers require 4-post enclosed cabinets supporting both front and rear mounting rails; 2-post open-frame relay racks are for lightweight patch panels and network switches only.
  • Rack Hole Types: Square holes (modern enterprise standard—use snap-in cage nuts) vs. round threaded/unthreaded holes (older telecom racks).
  • Bottom-Heavy Weight Distribution: Always install heavy UPS units, disk shelves, and 4U servers at the bottom of the rack (`U1–U10`) to lower the center of gravity and prevent tip-over hazards.
  • Sliding Rails & Cable Management Arms (CMA): Allow a server to slide smoothly forward out of the rack for hot-swap maintenance without unplugging power or network cables.
  • Anti-Tip Stabilizer Feet & Bolt-Down: Extend front stabilizer feet or bolt cabinets to the slab floor before sliding heavy servers out on rails.

Crash Carts, KVM Switches & Structured Cabling

  • Crash Cart: Mobile rolling cart equipped with a monitor, USB keyboard/mouse, VGA/HDMI/DisplayPort adapters, and serial rollover cables wheeled directly up to a rack when network/OOB access is unavailable.
  • Local Rackmount KVM Switch (1U Console Tray): Folding LCD screen + keyboard + touchpad mounted inside the rack wired to 8–32 servers so one console switches between local servers at the rack.
  • KVM over IP: Encapsulates local keyboard, video, and mouse signals over Ethernet/IP for remote BIOS-level control on servers lacking built-in BMC cards.
  • Patch Panels & Bend Radius: Terminate horizontal structured cabling cleanly at rack patch panels; maintain proper fiber/copper bend radius and velcro ties (avoid over-tightened zip ties that crush CAT6/fiber jackets).
  • Voltage Input Safety: North America = 110–120V / 60Hz; UK/Europe = 220–240V / 50Hz; verify auto-switching PSUs before plugging in.
