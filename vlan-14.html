Asset Lifecycle, Docs & Compliance

Procurement to decommissioning, burn-in testing, CAB change control, CMDB/SOPs, NIST SP 800-88 sanitization, and regulatory compliance.

DOMAIN SCOPE (VLAN 14): Everything inside this room belongs strictly to Server Hardware Lifecycle Management, Change Control Governance, Operational Documentation, Data Sanitization Standards (NIST SP 800-88), and Regulatory Compliance.

Hardware Lifecycle & Change Management

  • Lifecycle Stages: Procurement & Receiving Inspection → Asset Tagging & CMDB Entry → Staging/Burn-In Testing → Production Deployment → Maintenance/Warranty Renewal → Decommissioning & Media Sanitization.
  • Burn-In Testing: Stress-testing new servers (CPU, RAM, storage, PSUs) for 24–72 hours prior to production cutover to catch early "infant mortality" component defects.
  • Change Management & CAB: Standard/Normal changes require a formal Request for Change (RFC), impact/risk assessment, Backout / Rollback Plan, maintenance window approval from the Change Advisory Board (CAB), and post-change verification.
  • Emergency Changes: Expedited approval path (eCAB) used only to resolve active critical outages or zero-day security incidents.

Operational Documentation & Baselines

  • CMDB (Configuration Management Database): Central repository tracking hardware serial numbers, warranty expirations, OS versions, IP assignments, and upstream/downstream application dependencies.
  • SOPs & Runbooks: Step-by-step Standard Operating Procedures ensuring repeatable execution of routine tasks, failover drills, and incident response.
  • Rack Elevation & Network Diagrams: Physical U-position rack maps, patch-panel port schedules, and logical Layer 2/Layer 3 topology diagrams kept updated after every move/add/change.
  • Performance & Configuration Baselines: Recorded snapshots of normal CPU, RAM, disk IOPS, and network utilization used to spot anomalies during future incidents.
NIST SP 800-88 Sanitization Tier Mechanism & Action Media & Reuse Eligibility
Clear Logical overwriting of addressable storage locations with standard write commands (zero-fill / multi-pass overwrite) Protects against simple keyboard/software recovery; media can be reused internally within the organization.
Purge Cryptographic Erase (destroying the media encryption key on SEDs), ATA Secure Erase, or Degaussing (magnetic platters/tape only) Protects against state-of-the-art laboratory recovery; note that degaussing does NOT work on SSDs/flash media.
Destroy Physical destruction via industrial shredding, pulverizing, incineration, or smelting Highest assurance; media is permanently destroyed and cannot be reused (mandatory for failed SSDs or top-secret data).

Compliance, Auditing & Data Sovereignty

  • Certificate of Destruction (CoD): Formal auditable chain-of-custody document recording drive serial numbers, destruction method, date, and witness signature when decommissioning storage media.
  • Data Sovereignty & Residency: Legal requirement dictating that regulated personal/financial data must remain physically stored within specific national or regional borders (e.g., UK/EU GDPR).
  • Regulatory Frameworks: PCI-DSS (cardholder payment environments), HIPAA (healthcare ePHI), SOX (financial audit integrity), and GDPR (personal data privacy & breach notification).
  • Service Level Agreements (SLAs): Contractual uptime guarantees (e.g., 99.99% = ~52.6 minutes max downtime/year) and vendor hardware replacement response tiers (e.g., 4-hour onsite parts).
