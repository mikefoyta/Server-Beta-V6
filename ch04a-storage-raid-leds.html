Storage Technologies, RAID Math & Drive LED Decoder

Interactive field guide untangling storage layers, SAS/SATA physical locks, U.3 tri-mode NVMe slots, live RAID capacity math, Exercise 4.1 carrier LED states, and exam scenario drills.

1. Storage Media (What Holds Bits)

  • HDD (Hard Disk Drive): Electromechanical spinning magnetic platters + actuator arm. High bulk capacity (8–10+ TB), lower cost/GB, sensitive to shock and magnetic degaussing.
  • SSD (Solid-State Drive): Non-volatile NAND flash memory chips with zero moving parts. Silent, shock-resistant, ultra-low latency, immune to magnetic erasure.

2. Protocol / Interface (How It Talks)

  • SATA: Serial ATA (6 Gbps). Pairs with both HDDs and SSDs.
  • SAS: Serial Attached SCSI (12–22.5 Gbps, full-duplex). Pairs with both HDDs and SSDs.
  • NVMe: Runs directly over PCIe lanes. SSD-only (spinning platters cannot saturate PCIe).

3. Physical Form Factor (The Shape)

  • 3.5" LFF (Large Form Factor): Up to 26.1 mm height; bulk enterprise HDDs.
  • 2.5" SFF (Small Form Factor): Up to 15 mm height; high-RPM SAS HDDs, SAS/SATA SSDs, and U.2/U.3 NVMe caddies.
  • M.2: Gum-stick PCB card on motherboard (non-hot-swappable).
SK0-006 Exam Trigger Media and Interface are independent layers: a SAS SSD and a SATA SSD both use NAND flash, whereas an NVMe drive is always an SSD communicating directly over the PCIe bus rather than through a legacy SAS/SATA controller.

SATA Drive → SAS Backplane = WORKS

  • A SATA drive plug has an open notch/gap between its power and data segments.
  • It slides straight into a universal SAS backplane receptacle without obstruction.
  • The SAS controller speaks both SCSI and ATA commands, operating the SATA drive at SATA link speed.

SAS Drive → SATA Backplane = BLOCKED

  • A SAS drive plug has a solid plastic bridge connecting its power and data pins (which carries secondary dual-port lanes).
  • Attempting to insert a SAS drive into a standard SATA receptacle hits the plastic divider—physically preventing connection.
  • Even with passive adapters, a SATA controller cannot speak SCSI.
SK0-006 Exam Trigger One-Way Compatibility: SAS controllers accept both SAS and SATA drives; SATA controllers accept SATA only. Furthermore, never mix SAS and SATA drives inside the same RAID virtual disk—keep arrays uniform by drive interface.

M.2 (Internal Stick)

  • Plugs directly into motherboard M.2 slot (or PCIe carrier card).
  • Not hot-swappable—requires opening the server chassis to service; commonly used for mirrored hypervisor boot drives.

U.2 (2.5" Hot-Swap NVMe)

  • Standard 2.5" SFF hot-swappable server caddy using an SFF-8639 connector carrying up to 4 PCIe lanes.
  • Dedicated U.2 bays accept NVMe drives only (not legacy SAS/SATA).

U.3 (Universal Tri-Mode Bay)

  • Next-gen 2.5" SFF backplane wiring paired with a Tri-Mode HBA/RAID controller.
  • Accepts SAS, SATA, and NVMe drives interchangeably in the exact same physical front drive bay.
SK0-006 Exam Trigger If an exam scenario asks how to deploy hot-swappable NVMe storage in front-accessible 2.5" server bays without shutting down the host, choose U.2 or U.3—never M.2.

Internal SAS Breakout ("Octopus") Cable

  • HBA / Controller End: Uses a 4-lane internal connector such as SFF-8484 (or Mini-SAS SFF-8087 / SFF-8643).
  • Drive End: Fans out into four individual SFF-8482 drive plugs (each combining data + separate power feed).

External SAS & Serial Advantages

  • External Connector: Uses SFF-8470 (4-lane external cable secured with thumbscrews/jackscrews) to link external JBOD enclosures.
  • Point-to-Point Serial: Unlike legacy parallel multidrop SCSI, SAS uses point-to-point serial links (up to 22.5 Gbps on SAS-4) and requires no bus termination.

Because SSDs store charge in NAND cells rather than magnetic domains, magnetic degaussing has zero effect on SSDs—use Cryptographic Erase, ATA Secure Erase, or physical shredding.

External SATA (eSATA)

  • Provides native SATA speed externally using more robust, shielded connectors and cables up to 2 meters.
  • Critical Limitation: Standard eSATA cables carry data only—no electrical power (requires an external PSU or hybrid eSATAp port).

USB Attached SCSI (UAS / UASP)

  • Protocol allowing external storage enclosures over USB 3.0+ to communicate using native SCSI command queues rather than slower legacy USB Mass Storage Bulk-Only Transport (BOT).
  • Enables command queuing and dramatically lowers CPU overhead during large external backups.
SK0-006 Exam Trigger If a technician plugs an external eSATA drive into a server's standard eSATA port and the drive fails to spin up or power on, remember that standard eSATA does not supply power over the data cable.

Rebuild Risk Trap

Why RAID 6 Beats RAID 5 on Multi-TB Drives

Rebuilding a failed 10 TB HDD in RAID 5 hammers every remaining disk at 100% read load for hours. If a second disk hits an Unrecoverable Read Error (URE) or dies mid-rebuild, the entire array collapses. RAID 6's dual parity survives that second failure.

High-IOPS OLTP Trap

Why Databases Prefer RAID 10 Over RAID 5/6

Parity arrays (RAID 5/6) incur a 4x to 6x write penalty (read data, read parity, calculate new parity, write data, write parity). RAID 10 avoids parity calculations completely, making it the top exam answer for write-heavy databases.
